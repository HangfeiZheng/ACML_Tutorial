Tutorial Overview

Real-world data are often affected by outliers, heavy-tailed noise, and distributional contamination. Classical statistical learning methods based on maximum likelihood estimation (MLE) can be highly sensitive to such deviations from the assumed probabilistic model.

Existing approaches to robust statistical learning include replacing quadratic losses with robust alternatives (e.g., $\ell_1$$-loss, Huber and Tukey losses), adopting distributionally robust optimization, assuming heavy-tailed noise distributions, or applying external outlier detection. While these approaches offer different forms of robustness, they often rely on specific loss designs, distributional assumptions or additional procedures for identifying outliers. **Divergence-based estimation offers a principled probabilistic framework** that incorporates robustness directly into statistical inference, adaptively reducing the influence of contaminated observations while retaining useful information, **without requiring explicit assumptions about the contamination distribution or separate outlier detection**.

This tutorial provides a systematic introduction to robust statistical learning, starting from classical robust loss functions and maximum likelihood estimation (MLE) and establishing their connections to divergence-based estimation. With particular emphasis on $\alpha$-divergence, we introduce a unified probabilistic framework for robust learning that incorporates adaptive probabilistic weighting to mitigate contamination. We further discuss the robustness–efficiency tradeoff, optimization strategies, and theoretical properties of the resulting estimators.

We further illustrate how these ideas can be applied to a widely range of practical learning problems, including dimension reduction, representation learning, classification, tracking, image and signal processing.

By the end, you will be able to

  • Understand the limitations of classical estimation under contamination.
  • Explain the connections between maximum likelihood and divergence-based estimation.
  • Interpret the robustness mechanism induced by α-divergence.
  • Understand optimization strategies and theoretical properties of robust estimators.
  • Recognize practical applications of divergence-based robust learning.

Who it is for

Researchers, students, and practitioners in machine learning, statistics, and signal processing. A basic background in probability and statistical learning is helpful, but prior knowledge of divergence-based robust estimation is not required.
